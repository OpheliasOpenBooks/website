# Jacques the Fatalist and His Master — Denis Diderot (1796)

**Original title:** *Jacques le fataliste et son maître*. Written during the later eighteenth century, circulated privately before publication, first published posthumously in Paris by Buisson in 1796 (French Revolutionary year V). Bibliothèque nationale de France's catalogue confirms 1796 first complete French edition.

**Complete French text now hosted:** The entire original French narrative is locally hosted in 23 parts, `public/books/jacques-le-fataliste-original-francais-part-1.json` through `...-23.json`, read-only via `jacques-le-fataliste-original-francais.html`. Source: [Project Gutenberg #39976](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/39976), from the nineteenth-century Jules Assézat edition; local reading body transcribed from [GITenberg 39976-0.txt](https://github.com/GITenberg/Jacques-le-fataliste-et-son-ma-tre_39976/blob/master/39976-0.txt), lines 352 through 14143 of that text (starting with the book title and 'Comment s'étaient-ils rencontrés?' and ending with 'et qu'il s'endormait.'). The earlier editorial 'Notice préliminaire' by Jules Assézat is excluded as later editorial commentary, not Diderot's novel. The literary text and ending are preserved, including inline references/notes; reflowing paragraph whitespace is the only material display normalization. Automated source equality check ignoring whitespace passed. Approximately 89,300 words in all hosted parts. The 23 divisions are loading parts, not original chapters. No narration controls or French TTS are offered, in keeping with the library's original-language companion policy. The manuscript is **not** claimed to follow the exact typography or textual variants of the 1796 first edition.

**Historic art:** The actual frontispiece from the 1796 first edition, [Wikimedia Commons 'Jacques le Fataliste 1796.png'](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jacques_le_Fataliste_1796.png), public domain from the Bibliothèque nationale de France. The linked image is a historical frontispiece, not a bound front-cover photograph or a new illustration.

**English translation status — not hosted:** An anonymous public-domain English translation was printed in London in 1797 under the title *James the Fatalist and His Master* (G. G. and J. Robinson, three volumes). The [Wikimedia Commons scan of the English translation](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:James_the_Fatalist_and_His_Master_1797.djvu) is a reference, but we have **not** located and validated a clean, complete machine-readable English text for all three volumes and so have not uploaded a purported unabridged English audiobook. Modern twentieth-century English translations have separate copyright protections and have not been copied.
