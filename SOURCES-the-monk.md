# The Monk — Matthew Gregory Lewis (1796)

**First edition:** *The Monk: A Romance*, anonymously published in London by J. Bell in 1796, three volumes. Later author attributions use M. G. Lewis / Matthew Gregory Lewis.

**Source:** [Project Gutenberg ebook 601](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/601), also hosted in [GITenberg/The-Monk--A-Romance_601](https://github.com/GITenberg/The-Monk--A-Romance_601), plain English source `601.txt`, release 1996, updated March 2015, transcribed from historical public-domain print materials. No claim is made that every editorial reading and punctuation decision corresponds exactly to the 1796 first issue; the hosted text is a faithful reading transcription, not a page facsimile.

**Completeness:** Author’s opening Latin epigraph and accompanying English verse; Lewis’s original prefatory poem (dated The Hague, 28 October 1794); author's advertisement identifying borrowed ballad traditions; all **three original volumes** and all **twelve numbered chapters** (vol. I: 3, vol. II: 4, vol. III: 5), with their original short quotations/poems, narrative ballads, and complete final passage ending *Corse of the despairing Monk.* Included text is approximately 137,000 words. OCR-like typographical transcription remains as provided by Gutenberg; paragraph boundaries preserved and prose line breaks reflowed; verse retains line breaks where detectable. Header/footer Project Gutenberg licensing boilerplate is not part of the novel and excluded.

**Hosting:** `the-monk.html`; 24 local reading JSON files `public/books/the-monk-part-1.json`–`public/books/the-monk-part-24.json`. The first reading part contains title, epigraph, preface and advertisement; subsequent reading parts divide the twelve chapters by paragraph boundaries for lighter page loads; these 24 groups are **not** new original chapters. Original publication was English; no translation or read-only companion is needed. The familiar reader supports browser/device English narration, per-book resume and bookmarks, page navigation, auto-turn, adjustable text, night mode, and library share UI through `ophelia-narration.js` and `ophelia-share.js`. No recorded audiobook.
