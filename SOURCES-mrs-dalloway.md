# Sources — *Mrs. Dalloway* (1925), Virginia Woolf

## Edition and public-domain status
- Virginia Woolf (1882–1941), *Mrs. Dalloway*, first published 1925 by the Hogarth Press (London) and Harcourt, Brace (New York).
- The text is in the public domain in the United States because it was published in 1925. Project Gutenberg eBook 71865 explicitly confirms U.S. public-domain status: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/71865
- Local complete source: `public/books/mrs-dalloway-woolf-1925.txt`, copied from the public GitHub preservation mirror https://github.com/GITenberg/Mrs-Dalloway_71865/blob/master/71865-0.txt of Project Gutenberg eBook 71865, which was created by Carla Foust, Emmanuel Ackerman and the Online Distributed Proofreading Team.
- This transcription follows the printed American edition and includes the publisher's historical front matter and the Project Gutenberg transcriber's note. Its front matter says “Twenty-third printing”; it must not be mistaken for a 1925 first printing. The American and British 1925 texts can differ in minor readings, so this reader is explicitly the Gutenberg American-text transcription, not a facsimile of the British first edition.
- The book's own story has **no numbered chapters**. The reader preserves a single continuous story, with separate selectable *Front matter* and *Transcriber's note* sections for navigation.
- First novel line: “Mrs. Dalloway said she would buy the flowers herself.”
- Final story lines: “It is Clarissa, he said. / For there she was.” followed by “THE END”.
- Original work: approximately 63,426 whitespace-delimited words in the source prose. The reader checks the complete original opening, ending, and minimum text length.
