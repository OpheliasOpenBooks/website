# The Canterbury Tales — source assessment (not yet catalogued)

## Editorial requirement
Requested work: *The Canterbury Tales* by Geoffrey Chaucer (c. 1387–1400), **entire surviving work, unabridged, in legible modern English** for Ophelia’s Open Book, with Alice-style browser read-aloud, automatic page turning, bookmarks, and literary sharing.

**Publishing status (12 November 2026): source review only.** Do not add this title to the live catalog or describe it as published until a complete modern-English edition with suitable republication and read-aloud rights is verified. Chaucer's medieval text itself is in the public domain, but copyright in a modern translation is a separate question.

## Verified potential sources and limitations
1. **Project Gutenberg #22120**, `https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/22120` — Walter W. Skeat's scholarly complete edition of the surviving *Canterbury Tales*; **Middle English**, not a modern-English translation. Useful as the full-text completeness baseline. All surviving work, not the 120 tales Chaucer once planned.
2. **Project Gutenberg #2383**, `https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2383` — D. Laing Purves, modernized orthography (19th century), not fully modern English; his preface expressly states that *The Tale of Melibœus* and *The Parson's Tale* were condensed by roughly 30 pages in total. **Not an unabridged source.**
3. **Project Gutenberg #79952**, `https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/79952` — John S. P. Tatlock and Percy MacKaye, *The Complete Poetical Works of Geoffrey Chaucer, Now First Put Into Modern English* (1912), modern-English prose, U.S. public domain. Editors explicitly report omissions of passages for coarseness and summary treatment of both *Melibee* and *The Parson's Tale*. **Readable but not unabridged; do not silently use it as the entire work.**
4. **Percy MacKaye**, *The Canterbury Tales of Geoffrey Chaucer* (1914), `https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Canterbury_Tales_of_Geoffrey_Chaucer` — modern prose rendering of the General Prologue and **ten** tales only; not complete.
5. **A. S. Kline**, complete modernisation (2007): `https://www.poetryintranslation.com/PITBR/English/Canthome.php`. Reader-friendly and covers the entire surviving sequence including Melibee and the Parson's Tale, but **copyright © 2007, All Rights Reserved**. Copyright notice `https://www.poetryintranslation.com/Admin/Copyright.php` allows only defined noncommercial textual reproductions and requires permission for public performances, sound recordings, and commercial uses. It is **not public-domain text** and cannot automatically be rehosted for the existing free-read-and-listen website.
6. **Ronald L. Ecker and Eugene J. Crook** (1993), *The Canterbury Tales: A Complete Translation into Modern English*, full modern English but still copyrighted, including registered claim for translation; needs rights clearance before republication.
7. **Harvard Chaucer resources**, `https://chaucer.fas.harvard.edu/pages/text-and-translations` — valuable interlinear translation resource; full hosted translations are not automatically reusable as public-domain editions.

## What is needed before publication
- Identify a truly unabridged, clearly modern-English translation of the surviving collection in the U.S. public domain, or obtain a suitable, permanent grant to reproduce the **complete** modern-English text and use it in browser speech synthesis. Do not copy a copyrighted translation merely because it is freely viewable on another site.
- Verify all 24 extant tales, their prologues, interludes, and Chaucer's Retraction, retaining known unfinished tales in their authentic unfinished form.
- Confirm full prose versions of *Melibee* and *The Parson's Tale*, as well as all passages omitted for explicit language in some adaptations.
- Use the shared `public/js/ophelia-narration.js` and `public/js/ophelia-share.js`, all reader preferences, and an appropriate public-domain historic cover/illustration, only after text rights and completeness are established.

This note protects the library's **unabridged public-domain** standard; it is not a substitute for adding a readable edition.
