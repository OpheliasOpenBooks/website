# Sources — A Room of One’s Own (1929), Virginia Woolf

## Publication and public domain
- Virginia Woolf (1882–1941), A Room of One’s Own, first published in 1929 by the Hogarth Press in London, expanding talks given at Newnham and Girton in October 1928.
- The 1929 original is in the U.S. public domain, having been published before 1931. Rights outside the United States can differ.
- First-edition bibliographic record: https://www.themorgan.org/printed-books/417538
- Original historical edition reference: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Room_of_One%27s_Own_(Hogarth_1929)

## Text source and extent
- The complete six-chapter essay plus the opening author’s note has been preserved without abridgment.
- Text sourced from Project Gutenberg Australia 0200791.txt, produced by Col Choat, first posted 2002, updated April 2020: https://gutenberg.net.au/ebooks02/0200791.txt
- Archival copy retrieved from: https://github.com/sceckert/Data-and-Literary-Study-Spring2022/blob/master/_week2/woolf-a-room-of-ones-own.txt
- This is an electronic transcription, not a first-printing facsimile. Its editorial notes explain that italicized words were converted to uppercase and footnotes retained in square brackets.
- The six chapters contain approximately 37,706 whitespace-delimited words, including the complete original final passage. No chapter is omitted.
- Local data files: public/books/a-room-of-ones-own-1929-01.json, public/books/a-room-of-ones-own-1929-02.json, public/books/a-room-of-ones-own-1929-03.json.
- Clear transcription errors corrected only in opening note and display title: 'Newnharn' to 'Newnham', and missing apostrophe in title. The six original chapter texts have not been rewritten.
- The first chapter opens 'But, you may say, we asked you to speak about women and fiction'. The essay ends with 'and that so to work, even in poverty and obscurity, is worth while.' followed by THE END.
