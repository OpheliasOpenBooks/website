# A Princess of Mars — source and editorial notes

**Reader:** `a-princess-of-mars.html`  
**Author:** Edgar Rice Burroughs  
**Publication:** First serialized as *Under the Moons of Mars* in *The All-Story*, February–July 1912; first book edition, *A Princess of Mars*, A. C. McClurg & Co., Chicago, 1917.  
**Copyright:** The original book and its 1917 Frank E. Schoonover cover art are public domain in the United States. No modern edition, adaptation, or translation has been reproduced.

**Unabridged text:** Project Gutenberg eBook #62, `https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/62`, fetched from its GITenberg transcribed archive, `https://github.com/GITenberg/A-Princess-of-Mars_62/blob/master/62.txt`. The text is **hosted locally** as `public/books/a-princess-of-mars/manifest.json` and ten numbered part files. All **28 original chapters**, original **foreword**, and **dedication ("To My Son Jack")** are preserved in sequence. The original source's table of Project Gutenberg distribution terms, Gutenberg production headers and footer, and frontispiece extraction labels are not narrated; narrative text is unchanged apart from joining line-wraps.

**Historic artwork:** Authentic *A Princess of Mars* cover by Frank E. Schoonover, 1917 A. C. McClurg first edition, reproduced at the U.S. Library of Congress page `https://read.gov/books/johncarter.html` and image `https://read.gov/books/images/princess_mars.jpg`. The same original cover is documented at `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Princess_of_Mars.jpg`, marked U.S. public domain. This is **not** a generated cover or a later reissue.

**Reader implementation:** Reuses the site-approved Ophelia narrated reader architecture and `public/js/ophelia-narration.js`, with selectable chapters, voice/speed, 0.5–2× pace, pause/resume, saved bookmarks and reading position, evening mode, auto-turn, and page navigation. English original; no translated-language panel. The historical book-cover art is shown as the opening cover, not presented as an illustration of Chapter I.

**Completeness validation:** 67,181 archived narrative words, 28 sequential chapters plus original foreword and dedication, independently verified across all ten hosted JSON parts. Final words: “I believe that they are waiting there for me, and something tells me that I shall soon know.”

Project Gutenberg is linked for bibliographic provenance, not as an outside reader. The text loads only from Ophelia's own repository files.
