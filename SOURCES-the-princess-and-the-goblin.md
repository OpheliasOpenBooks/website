# The Princess and the Goblin — source, rights and text

**Title:** *The Princess and the Goblin*, George MacDonald; first book publication Strahan & Co., 1872 (previously serialized in *Good Words for the Young*). U.S. public-domain original English novel.

**Edition used:** Project Gutenberg eBook #34339, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34339, transcribed from the David McKay Company 1920 illustrated historical edition (archival text https://github.com/GITenberg/The-Princess-and-the-Goblin_34339/blob/master/34339.txt). All 32 original chapters I–XXXII included. Paragraphs and dialogue retained in order; prose linewraps joined, verse lines kept, Project Gutenberg headers, illustration placeholders and transcription notes excluded from narration.

**Completeness:** 51115 whitespace-delimited narrative words; begins “THERE was once a little princess who--” and preserves the final author's dialogue “I won't say that either, now.” The full narrative is distributed in eight locally hosted JSON reading parts (four chapters per part): `public/books/the-princess-and-the-goblin/manifest.json` and `part-001.json` through `part-008.json`.

**Period artwork:** Historical 1920 illustrated edition cover and eight Jessie Willcox Smith illustrations from https://github.com/GITenberg/The-Princess-and-the-Goblin_34339/tree/master/34339-h/images. These are public-domain period illustrations, explicitly *not* attributed to Arthur Hughes (illustrator of the original 1872 edition). Presented at the opening or selected related chapters rather than reproduced as a facsimile.

**Reader:** `the-princess-and-the-goblin.html`. Existing narrated Ophelia reading interface with shared `public/js/ophelia-narration.js`, device TTS, adjustable voice and speed, bookmarks, saved reading place, chapter navigation, auto-turn, text size and evening theme. Original English, so no original-language translation panel.

**Checks:** 32 chapter boundaries, opening and closing text, total word count of 51115, all eight part word counts, and inline JavaScript syntax. Live browser rendering, image availability and playback must still be tested.
