# An Essay on Man — Alexander Pope (1733–1734)

## Complete poem and editorial source
- **Four epistles** published individually in 1733–1734 (Epistles I–III in 1733, Epistle IV in January 1734); the full set was collected in 1734. Original English text is public domain in the U.S. Bibliographic references: [University of Toronto RPO](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/essay-man-epistle-i) and [Wikisource bibliography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/An_Essay_on_Man).
- Text from [Project Gutenberg #2428](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2428), the [GITenberg transcription](https://github.com/GITenberg/An-Essay-on-Man--Moral-Essays-and-Satires_2428/blob/master/2428.txt), based on Henry Morley's **1891 Cassell & Company edition**, *An Essay on Man; Moral Essays and Satires*. This reading text **is not claimed to be a direct copy of the 1733–1734 first printings**.
- Includes Pope's own prose preface **“The Design”** (four paragraphs), the **Argument of each epistle**, and all **four complete epistles**, 1304 verse lines and 10181 poem words, with original printed line breaks and spelling. Section lengths: Epistle I 294 lines; Epistle II 294 lines; Epistle III 318 lines; Epistle IV 398 lines.
- *The Universal Prayer* (separately dated 1738 in the editor's introduction), *Moral Essays*, and *Satires* appear nearby in the Morley anthology but are **distinct poems** and not part of this four-epistle work. They are intentionally excluded rather than being misrepresented as epistles.
- Verse display preserves original line breaks; very long stanza blocks are divided for pagination only at existing complete lines, not during verse. No stanzas, lines, epistles, or argumentative material omitted. The shared browser narrator speaks “Epistle One” through “Epistle Four” for printed Roman headings.
