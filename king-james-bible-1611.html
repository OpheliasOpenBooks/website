A Note on Reading the Scriptures

Out of respect for the historic language of the 1611 King James Bible and for readers who hold this text sacred, Ophelia’s Open Book presents this edition without automated narration. This edition preserves the original 1611 English wording and historical spelling as represented in its transcription. This is Early Modern English, sometimes informally called old English, but distinct from the much earlier language scholars call Old English. Its unfamiliar spellings, names, and expressions may be pronounced inaccurately by modern computer-generated voices. Rather than risk misrepresenting the words, we invite readers to encounter the text directly, at their own pace, with its historical wording preserved.

This is an editorial choice made in a spirit of care and reverence, not a judgment on the many meaningful traditions of reading Scripture aloud.

Historical edition and sources

The King James Bible was first printed by Robert Barker in 1611. Its original presentation includes the Old Testament, books traditionally called the Apocrypha, and the New Testament. Later printings commonly differ in spelling and contents. The engraving pictured here is the actual 1611 title page, not an imagined book jacket.

The two original prefatory writings are also available in Ophelia’s reading edition of the 1611 dedication and translators’ preface.

Original-spelling, chapter-and-verse transcription: Bible-kjv-1611 (80-book text collection), with each book stored locally in Ophelia's library. For the original introductory writings, typography and marginal material, consult the 1611 King James Bible on Wikisource. Transcriptions can differ from original printed copies; this reader does not claim to be a diplomatic facsimile.
