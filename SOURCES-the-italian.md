# The Italian — Ann Radcliffe (1797)

**Original title:** *The Italian; or, The Confessional of the Black Penitents. A Romance.* First issued anonymously? The surviving first-edition title page specifically says **Ann Radcliffe**. The printed first edition is **dated 1797** (sometimes documented as published late 1796). London: T. Cadell Jr. and W. Davies, three volumes. The complete work contains **34 numbered chapters**, distributed as Volume I (11), Volume II (10), and Volume III (13), plus the opening frame narrative and its original epigraph.

**Text provenance and edition:** All original English. Volumes I–II from public-domain Project Gutenberg texts [74643](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/74643) and [74640](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/74640), mirrored in GITenberg. Volume III sourced from public-domain [Faded Page 20250925](https://www.fadedpage.com/showbook.php?pid=20250925), a 2025 transcription of the 1797 printing prepared by Distributed Proofreaders Canada. Original public-domain literary text retained; source files are not necessarily an identical editorial version and typography is lightly reflowed. Gutenberg reports some spelling regularization; Faded Page reports correction of printer errors. These are *reading transcriptions*, not facsimiles. Authorial text is unabridged, including the original title epigraph, the prologue, verse chapter epigraphs and in-text poems, complete dialogue and ending. The note '[1] O happy day! O happy day!' occurs in Vol. III, Chapter XII and is retained.

**Hosted book:** 29 local reading parts in `public/books/the-italian-part-1.json` through `public/books/the-italian-part-29.json`, ~172,000 words. Reading parts 1–9 encompass Volume I; 10–18 Volume II; 19–29 Volume III. A few lengthy chapters are split at paragraph boundaries to keep reading parts manageable. Parts are editorial loading units, not new chapter numbers. Unabridged English reading room: `the-italian.html`. Standard shared narration `ophelia-narration.js` and sharing `ophelia-share.js`, page bookmarks, auto-turn, device voices, resume position and text size/night settings. English is the original language, hence no separate translated companion panel.
