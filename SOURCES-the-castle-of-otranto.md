# The Castle of Otranto — Horace Walpole (1764)

## Text and rights
- Horace Walpole, *The Castle of Otranto*, first issued December 1764 with a title page dated 1765; public domain in the United States.
- The **edition reproduced** is the 1901 Cassell & Company / *Cassell's National Library* text edited by Henry Morley, transcribed by David Price for Project Gutenberg #696: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/696 ; checked against the GITenberg plain-text mirror: https://github.com/GITenberg/The-Castle-of-Otranto_696/blob/master/696-0.txt .
- The local full text is `public/books/the-castle-of-otranto-walpole-1901.json`. It has nine ordered reader sections: title, Henry Morley's 1901 introduction, Walpole's first-edition preface, the sonnet to Lady Mary Coke, and chapters I–V. The five fiction chapters alone contain approximately 34,569 words: 7,809, 7,480, 7,115, 6,416 and 5,749 respectively. The opening is "Manfred, Prince of Otranto..." and the final paragraph ends "the melancholy that had taken possession of his soul."
- This **is not presented as a facsimile of the first edition**. Historical publication matter or editorial supplements not in the 1901 Cassell reprint (such as the second-edition preface) are not silently claimed to be present.
- The language of composition is English. The first-edition cover's purported Italian authorship and translator "William Marshal" were a deliberate literary fiction; **do not add an original Italian text link** to Ophelia's original-language panel.
- Source line-wraps were joined, indentation normalized and Gutenberg distribution boilerplate omitted. The sonnet's verse line breaks are preserved. No original chapter text is summarized or omitted.
