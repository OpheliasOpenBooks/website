# Joseph Andrews — Henry Fielding (1742)

## Work and edition

*The History of the Adventures of Joseph Andrews and of his Friend Mr. Abraham Adams* was first published anonymously in London by Andrew Millar in **1742**, in two physical volumes. The novel has **four books and 64 chapters** (Book I: 18; Book II: 17; Book III: 13; Book IV: 16), preceded by Fielding's **Author's Preface**.

This website hosts the full novel as one navigable reader, not a condensed edition. The online transcription comes from a later edition **edited by George Saintsbury**, in the series *The Works of Henry Fielding*. Its editorial note states that the narrative text principally follows the **1762 first collected edition**, with minor editorial corrections. It is **not** represented as a verbatim reproduction of the 1742 first printing. The reader preserves Saintsbury's **General Introduction** and **Note to General Introduction** as a distinct front-matter section. Fielding's own preface follows separately.

Includes Book II chapters 14–17, Book III (13 chapters), and Book IV (16 chapters), ending in the original `THE END.`.
- Both text files are public-domain material in the United States. This project uses a locally hosted reading copy rather than depending on another site's availability at run time. Project Gutenberg eBook notices, reprint series contents tables, duplicated bibliographic title pages, and Gutenberg license boilerplate are not treated as part of Fielding's novel. The later editor's prose is clearly identified.

## Local text storage and preservation

- `public/books/joseph-andrews-fielding-volume-1.json` — 33 sections (editorial introduction, authorial preface, and 31 narrative chapters).
- `public/books/joseph-andrews-fielding-volume-2.json` — 33 sections (remaining 33 narrative chapters).
- `joseph-andrews.html` reads both files in original literary order; the two printed volumes are presented as one continuous novel.
- In total, the transcription contains approximately **134,957 whitespace-separated words**, including the editor's introduction and Fielding's preface. Automatic pagination adds no authored headings. Book and chapter labels remain preserved in the prose and Roman numerals are converted to spoken numbers only during audio preparation.
- Original chapter descriptions and paragraph boundaries are retained. Mechanical plain-text line wraps were joined within paragraphs; underscores used to mark print italics were removed without deleting the words. Text transcription and later Saintsbury-era spelling are otherwise retained. The closing `END OF VOL. I` and `THE END.` remain in their respective volumes. The formatting of tabular matter may differ from the original typesetting.
