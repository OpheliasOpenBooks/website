Hiroshima

John Hersey · Originally published in The New Yorker, 1946 · Expanded in 1985

Literary reference · Copyrighted work

On August 6, 1945, an atomic bomb was dropped on Hiroshima. John Hersey recounts the event and its aftermath through the experiences of six survivors, allowing their individual lives to convey the human consequences of nuclear warfare.

Originally published as an entire issue of The New Yorker in August 1946, the narrative later appeared as a book. In 1985, Hersey added a concluding chapter, The Aftermath, following the survivors decades later.

Why it belongs in this collection

Hersey’s restrained, eyewitness-centered approach is a landmark of narrative journalism. The book places human lives at the center of a historical event, and its influence extends beyond war reporting to questions of memory, civilian suffering, and the responsibilities of storytelling.

Publication history

1946: The original work appeared in The New Yorker and was subsequently issued in book form by Alfred A. Knopf. 1985: Hersey revisited the lives of survivors and contributed The Aftermath to an expanded edition.
